Florida Tree Stumps and Grinding Difficulty

Species can influence the work, but the actual stump and site matter more than the name alone.

Updated August 4, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Quick answer: Jacksonville-area stumps vary in density, moisture, decay, root flare and surface-root spread. A live oak, pine, palm or cypress should be quoted from the actual stump—not a species label alone.

Oak stumps

Live oak, water oak and laurel oak can develop broad root flare and large surface roots. The work area may extend well beyond the flat cut surface.

Pine stumps

Pine stumps can vary greatly by age, resin, decay and soil conditions. An isolated pine in open access may be straightforward, while a mature stump with buried obstacles is not.

Palm stumps

Palms have a different fibrous structure than hardwood trees, but size, moisture, access and cleanup still affect the job. “Palm” does not automatically mean a fixed price.

Cypress and wet areas

Bald cypress and other stumps in damp or low areas may involve soft ground, water, knees or access limitations that change equipment planning.

Crepe myrtle and regrowth concerns

Some species may produce sprouts from surviving roots. Discuss regrowth concerns separately because grinding the central stump does not remove every root.

Frequently asked questions

Is hardwood always more expensive?

Not always. Root flare, access, quantity and cleanup can outweigh species alone.

Should the species be included in the photo quote?

Yes, when known, but measurements and site photos remain essential.
