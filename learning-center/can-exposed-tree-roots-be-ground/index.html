Root Grinding

Can Exposed Tree Roots Be Ground Down?

Roots from a removed tree and structural roots from a living tree are not the same job.

Updated August 4, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Quick answer: Accessible roots from a removed tree can often be added to the grinding scope. Large roots attached to a living tree may affect health or stability and should not be cut automatically.

Roots from a removed tree

Surface roots and root flare may interfere with mowing, sod, fence lines, pathways and landscape grading. Photograph and mark every root that must be included because stump grinding does not automatically cover roots extending across the yard.

Roots from a living tree

Major roots help anchor and support a living tree. Cutting roots near the trunk, on the side of a lean or along a major structural path may weaken the tree. An arborist review may be appropriate before work.

Roots beneath hardscape

A grinder cannot reach roots underneath intact concrete, pavers, pool decks or structures. The overlying surface generally must be removed before the root is accessible.

Better alternatives for living roots

Depending on the problem, mulch, adjusted landscaping, a raised path or another root-friendly design may be safer than cutting a major root.

Frequently asked questions

Can root grinding stop mower damage?

It may help when the roots belong to a removed tree and are accessible within the planned work zone.

Does root grinding remove the full network?

No. It targets visible and reachable roots only.
