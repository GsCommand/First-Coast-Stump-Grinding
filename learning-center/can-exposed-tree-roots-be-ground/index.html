Roots and Grinding Depth

Can Exposed Tree Roots Be Ground Down?

Roots from a removed tree and structural roots from a living tree are not the same job and should not be treated the same way.

Updated August 5, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Written for root-grinding and landscape decisions across Jacksonville and Northeast Florida.

Quick answer: Accessible roots from a removed tree can often be included in a grinding scope. Major roots connected to a living tree should not be cut automatically because they may affect the tree’s health or stability.

Removed-tree roots versus living-tree roots

Root typeTypical approach
Root from a removed treeMay be ground when visible, reachable and included in the quote.
Root flare from a removed stumpOften included with the central stump if accessible.
Major root from a living treeRequires caution and may need arborist review.
Root beneath intact hardscapeCannot be reached until the overlying surface is removed.

When exposed roots from a removed tree may be ground

Visible roots can interfere with mowing, sod, fence lines, pathways and landscape grading. Photograph and mark every root that needs to be included because standard stump grinding does not automatically cover roots extending across the yard.

Why living-tree roots require more caution

Major roots help anchor a living tree and move water and nutrients. Cutting roots near the trunk, on the side of a lean or along a major structural path can increase stress or reduce stability.

When arborist review makes sense

  • The root belongs to a living tree
  • The root is large and close to the trunk
  • The tree leans toward a house, road or pool
  • Several roots would need to be cut
  • The tree already shows decline, decay or instability
  • The work is tied to construction near the tree

Roots beneath concrete, pavers and pool decks

A grinder cannot reach roots beneath intact hardscape or structures. The overlying material usually has to be removed first, and the next contractor may need to coordinate the excavation and restoration.

Safer alternatives for living roots

ProblemPossible alternative
Mower contactConvert the area to mulch or adjust the mowing boundary.
Uneven walkwayConsider a raised or rerouted path rather than cutting a major root.
Landscape gradeUse root-friendly design rather than burying or cutting structural roots.
Fence conflictAdjust post spacing or alignment when practical.

What to photograph for a root review

  • Close photo of the exposed root
  • Wide photo showing the entire tree or removed stump
  • Distance from the root to the trunk
  • Nearby structures, pavement and utilities
  • The exact section that must be cleared
  • The intended use after grinding

Frequently asked questions

Can exposed roots from a removed tree be ground?

Often, yes, when the roots are visible, reachable and included in the written scope.

Can major roots from a living tree be ground?

They should not be cut automatically. Major roots can affect tree health or stability and may require arborist review.

Can roots under concrete or pavers be ground?

Not while the surface remains intact. The overlying hardscape usually must be removed before the root is accessible.

Does root grinding remove the entire root network?

No. It targets visible and reachable roots only.
