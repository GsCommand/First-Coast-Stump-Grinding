How to Prepare Your Yard for Stump Grinding

A clear route, identified utilities and a defined cleanup plan reduce delays, protect the property and help the work match the written quote.

Updated August 5, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Written for residential and commercial stump grinding projects across Northeast Florida.

Quick answer: Unlock and measure gates, clear the complete equipment route, move vehicles and outdoor items, secure pets, identify irrigation and private lines, confirm every included stump and decide how chips and restoration will be handled.

Good preparation starts before the equipment arrives. The goal is to create a safe, visible route from the unloading area to the stump and make sure the contractor has accurate information about the work zone.

Homeowner preparation checklist

  • Confirm the service date and access instructions
  • Unlock gates and remove padlocks
  • Measure the narrowest gate and route opening
  • Move vehicles away from unloading and debris areas
  • Remove hoses, toys, furniture, planters and trash cans
  • Secure pets and keep children away from the work zone
  • Identify irrigation, lighting, pool plumbing and other private systems
  • Mark every included stump and surface root
  • Discuss stump chips, haul-away and restoration
  • Avoid saturating the access route before service

What to do before, during and after the appointment

StageHomeowner responsibilities
Before arrivalClear access, move vehicles, unlock gates, secure pets and identify private systems.
At arrivalConfirm the included stump, roots, depth, access assumptions and chip plan.
During grindingKeep people and pets outside the controlled work and debris zone.
After grindingReview the completed scope and follow the agreed chip-removal or restoration plan.

Clear the complete access route

Remove anything movable between the unloading point and the stump. Common obstacles include garden hoses, toys, planters, furniture, decorations, grills, trash cans and low portable lighting.

Do not focus only on the gate. A narrow turn, step, drainage swale, air-conditioning unit or soft section of lawn can affect equipment access after the gate has been cleared.

Move vehicles and protect the unloading area

The equipment may need a clear location near the street or driveway for unloading and loading. Move vehicles away from that route and from the expected debris-control zone when requested.

Secure pets, children and visitors

Stump grinding equipment creates noise and can throw wood chips and soil. Pets should remain indoors or securely separated from the work area. Children and visitors should stay outside the controlled zone until the operator confirms the work is complete.

Identify utilities and private systems

Public utility markings may not identify irrigation, pool plumbing, landscape lighting, septic components, invisible fencing or customer-installed electrical lines. Tell the contractor about known private systems and show visible heads, boxes, valves and equipment.

SystemWhat to show
IrrigationSprinkler heads, valve boxes and known line routes near the stump or access path.
Pool systemsEquipment pad, plumbing routes, drains, lighting and deck penetrations.
Landscape lightingFixtures, transformers and known low-voltage wire paths.
Invisible fencingBoundary route, transmitter location and any recent repairs.
Septic or drainageKnown tanks, drain fields, cleanouts, swales and buried drainage components.

Avoid soaking the route before service

Turn off irrigation near the access route before the appointment when practical. Saturated soil can affect traction, ground protection and whether the machine can safely reach the stump.

Confirm the exact scope

Show the operator every stump and visible surface root included in the quote. For multiple stumps, number them in advance. This prevents a small stump or separate work area from being overlooked.

Decide what happens to the chips

Stump grinding produces a mixture of wood chips and soil. Confirm whether the material will be left in the hole, spread, piled or removed. Topsoil, grading, seed and sod should be identified as included or separate.

After-grinding planPreparation consideration
Leave chips onsiteChoose where excess material should be piled or spread.
Mulch bedConfirm the finish height and whether remaining chips are acceptable.
Grass or sodPlan to remove excess chips, add suitable soil, grade and allow for settlement.
Fence or hardscapeConfirm exact locations and whether standard grinding is sufficient for the next contractor.

Final five-minute walkthrough

  • Point out each included stump and root
  • Confirm the intended grinding depth
  • Review nearby private lines and structures
  • Confirm where chips will go
  • Ask what the finished area should look like

Frequently asked questions

Should sprinklers be turned off before stump grinding?

Avoid soaking the access route before service and identify sprinkler heads and irrigation lines near the stump.

Do vehicles need to be moved?

Move vehicles away from the unloading route, machine path and debris-control area when requested.

Should pets stay inside during stump grinding?

Yes. Pets, children and visitors should remain outside the controlled work zone while equipment is operating.

Do I need to remove stump chips before planting grass?

Usually, excess chips should be removed and replaced with suitable soil before grading, seed or sod. Restoration should be defined separately in the written scope.
