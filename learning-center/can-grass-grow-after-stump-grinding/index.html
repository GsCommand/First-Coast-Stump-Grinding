Aftercare

Can Grass Grow After Stump Grinding?

Yes—but the area needs soil preparation, not just seed on top of fresh chips.

Updated August 4, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Quick answer: Remove excess chips, add suitable topsoil, grade the area, then seed or sod. Expect some settlement as buried roots decay.

Why grass struggles in fresh grindings

Grinding debris contains a large amount of wood and loose material. It can settle unevenly and does not provide the same rooting environment as prepared topsoil.

A practical restoration sequence

  1. Remove excess chips and sawdust.
  2. Fill with suitable clean soil.
  3. Lightly settle and grade the area.
  4. Install seed or matching sod.
  5. Water according to the grass type and season.
  6. Check for settling and add soil later if needed.

Seed versus sod

Sod gives faster coverage and may match an established lawn more closely. Seed can be less expensive but may require more time and careful watering.

Why the area may sink later

Large roots remain underground after grinding. As they break down, the soil volume can change and create a low area that needs future top-dressing.

Frequently asked questions

Can the chips simply be covered with soil?

A thick layer of buried chips can continue settling. Removing excess material usually creates a better base.

Is lawn restoration included with grinding?

Only when the written scope includes chip removal, soil, grading and seed or sod.
