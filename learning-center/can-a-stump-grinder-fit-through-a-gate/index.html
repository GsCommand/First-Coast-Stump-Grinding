Access and Equipment

Can a Stump Grinder Fit Through a Residential Gate?

A gate can be wide enough while the rest of the route is still too narrow, steep or obstructed for safe equipment access.

Updated August 5, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Written for fenced residential properties in Jacksonville, St. Johns County, Clay County and Nassau County.

Quick answer: A stump grinder may fit through a residential gate when the narrowest clear opening and every point beyond it can accommodate the selected machine. Measure between fixed posts, hinges and latches, then photograph turns, slopes, steps, pavers, low branches and the working area around the stump.

Backyard stump grinding is an access problem before it becomes a grinding problem. A useful review follows the machine from the street or driveway all the way to the stump and back out again.

The complete access checklist

  • Street or driveway unloading area
  • Narrowest gate opening
  • Posts, hinges, latches and decorative trim
  • Side-yard width
  • Tight turns around buildings or equipment
  • Steps, slopes, drainage swales and retaining edges
  • Pavers, pool decks and soft lawn areas
  • Low branches, roof overhangs and utility equipment
  • Final working clearance around the stump
  • Exit route after the work is complete

How to measure a gate correctly

Measure the narrowest clear opening between fixed points. Do not rely on the nominal width of the gate panel. Hinges, latches, posts and trim can reduce the usable opening.

What to measureWhy it matters
Post to postShows the basic structural opening.
Hinge to latchMay reveal a narrower usable path than the post spacing.
Ground-level widthRoots, edging or raised pavers can reduce clearance near the tracks or tires.
Height clearanceLow branches, roof lines or overhead fixtures may interfere with the route.

The gate is only the first checkpoint

A machine may pass through the gate and then fail at a ninety-degree turn beside an air-conditioning unit, a narrow side yard, a step down to the pool deck or a soft drainage area. Photograph the entire route in sequence so the operator can understand the path before arrival.

Turns need more room than straight travel

Straight-line width does not fully describe a corner. The machine body, attachments and operator position may need additional room to turn without contacting walls, fences, landscaping or mechanical equipment.

Slopes, steps and drainage areas

Route conditionWhat to document
SlopePhotograph the grade from both directions and show nearby drop-offs or walls.
StepShow the height, edge and available approach. Do not assume equipment can safely cross it.
Drainage swaleShow depth, softness and whether the ground remains wet.
Pavers or pool deckShow the entire travel surface and any narrow borders or raised edges.
Soft lawnDocument saturated or recently irrigated areas that may affect traction or ground protection.

Working room around the stump

Reaching the stump is not enough. The cutter must move through the work area without contacting fences, walls, coping, sheds or other hard surfaces. Send a wide photo that shows the stump and everything around it, not only a close-up of the cut surface.

When fence-panel removal may help

Temporary panel removal can create a wider entrance or better turning angle. That work should be planned separately and approved by the property owner. The quote should state whether panel removal is excluded, owner-provided or handled by another contractor.

What to send for an access review

  • Gate photo with tape measure visible
  • Photo from outside looking in
  • Photo from inside looking out
  • Every turn between the gate and stump
  • Any step, slope or soft-ground section
  • Wide work-zone photo around the stump
  • ZIP code and intended use after grinding

Frequently asked questions

Is measuring the gate opening enough?

No. The complete route must be checked for hinges, latches, turns, slopes, steps, low branches, soft ground and fixed obstacles.

Where should I measure a residential gate?

Measure the narrowest clear opening between fixed points, including posts, hinges, latches and trim.

Can a stump grinder go over steps?

That depends on the machine and the actual route. Steps should be photographed before scheduling because they may prevent safe access.

Can removing a fence panel solve an access problem?

Sometimes, but removal and reinstallation should be planned and approved separately before service.
