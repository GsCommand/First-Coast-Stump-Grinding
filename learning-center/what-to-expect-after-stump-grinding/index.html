Chips and Lawn Restoration

What to Expect After Stump Grinding

The visible stump may be gone, but the final appearance depends on the agreed depth, chip handling and restoration scope.

Updated August 5, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Written for homeowners and property managers planning the next step after stump grinding.

Quick answer: Expect loose wood chips mixed with soil, deeper roots remaining underground and the possibility of future settlement. A finished lawn usually requires excess-chip removal, suitable topsoil, grading and seed or sod.

Stump grinding changes the site from a solid stump into a loose work area. What happens next should match the future use: mulch, lawn, fence, hardscape or construction.

What the area may look like immediately afterward

ResultWhat it means

Most underground roots remain

Routine stump grinding removes the central stump and accessible root flare. It does not excavate the entire underground root system. Deeper and lateral roots remain and gradually decompose.

Why settling can happen later

Two processes can lower the surface: loose grindings compact, and buried roots lose volume as they decay. Large stumps and broad root systems may create more noticeable settlement than small stumps.

What should be checked before the contractor leaves

  • Every included stump was addressed
  • Included surface roots were handled as written
  • The expected grinding depth was achieved where conditions allowed
  • Chips were left, spread, piled or removed as agreed
  • Nearby fences, pavers, irrigation and structures were reviewed
  • Any inaccessible wood or changed condition was explained
  • The next restoration step is clear

For lawn restoration

Remove excess wood material, add suitable topsoil and establish a stable grade before seed or sod. A thin layer of soil over deep chips can settle quickly and create a visible depression.

For hardscape or construction

Routine below-grade grinding is not automatically structural site preparation. Patios, driveways, pools, foundations and trenches may require additional excavation, soil compaction or review by the next contractor.

When to expect follow-up work

Monitor the area after rainfall and irrigation. Add soil when a low spot begins to form rather than waiting for a deep depression. Newly installed turf may also need patching if settlement occurs.

Frequently asked questions

Will there be a hole after stump grinding?

The grinding area is commonly filled with loose grindings unless the written scope includes chip removal, backfill and grading.

Do tree roots remain after stump grinding?

Yes. Most deeper and lateral roots remain underground and decompose gradually.

Can the ground settle after stump grinding?

Yes. Loose material compacts and buried roots decay, so the area may need future top-dressing.

Is the area ready for construction after routine stump grinding?

Not automatically. Patios, pools, foundations and utility trenches may require deeper excavation or separate site preparation.
