Hiring and Trust

Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Stump Grinding Company

The best comparison is not simply who gives the lowest number. It is which company provides the clearest, most complete written scope.

Updated August 5, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Written for homeowners, property managers and contractors comparing stump grinding companies in Northeast Florida.

Quick answer: Ask about current insurance, the exact stump and roots included, grinding depth, equipment access, chip handling, utilities, cleanup, payment timing and how hidden conditions are handled. Get those answers in writing before scheduling.

Stump grinding quotes can look similar while including very different work. One proposal may cover only the central stump. Another may include root flare, accessible surface roots, cleanup and a defined depth. Comparing the written scope protects both the customer and the contractor.

The 12 questions to ask

  • Is the company currently insured for this work?
  • Which stump or stumps are included?
  • Are the root flare and visible surface roots included?
  • What grinding depth is included?
  • What machine and access width are required?
  • Can the equipment handle the full route, including turns and slopes?
  • What happens to the stump chips?
  • Are haul-away, topsoil, grading, seed or sod included?
  • Who handles public utilities and private lines?
  • How are hidden rocks, metal or access changes handled?
  • When is payment due?
  • What exactly will the work area look like at completion?

1. Is the company currently insured?

Ask for current proof of liability coverage that applies to the scheduled work. A badge or general statement on a website is not the same as documentation. The name on the certificate should match the business performing the job.

2. Which stump and roots are included?

The proposal should identify every stump and explain whether the visible root flare or outward surface roots are included. “Stump grinding” does not automatically mean every exposed root across the yard will be removed.

Roots connected to a living tree require a different level of caution. Cutting major structural roots can affect tree health or stability, so an arborist may need to evaluate the condition before work proceeds.

3. What grinding depth is included?

Ask the contractor to connect the grinding depth to what you plan to do next. Grass, mulch, fencing, a patio and new construction do not always require the same scope.

Mulch or planting bedWill the remaining stump be low enough for the planned landscape finish?

4. Can the machine reach the stump?

Ask about the entire equipment route, not only the gate opening. Measure between posts and hinges, then show tight turns, slopes, steps, pavers, irrigation equipment and landscaping. Narrow-access equipment can help on fenced residential properties, but every route still needs to be reviewed.

5. What happens to the chips?

Stump grinding creates a mixture of wood chips and soil. Confirm whether that material will be left in the hole, spread, piled or hauled away. Also ask whether topsoil, grading, seed or sod are part of the quote or separate restoration work.

6. Who is responsible for utilities and private lines?

Public utility markings may not identify private irrigation, pool plumbing, low-voltage lighting, septic components or invisible fencing. Known private lines should be disclosed before work begins, and the quote should explain how unmarked conditions are handled.

7. How are hidden conditions handled?

Rocks, buried metal, old fence hardware, concrete, unexpected roots or soft ground may not be visible in photos. Ask whether the contractor will stop and discuss the issue, provide a change order or exclude the affected area.

8. What are the payment terms?

Payment timing should be clear before scheduling. The proposal should explain whether a deposit is required, when the balance is due and how approved changes are documented.

Red flags in a vague estimate

  • No written description of the included stump or roots
  • No grinding depth or future-use discussion
  • No mention of gate width or access route
  • No answer about chips or cleanup
  • No current insurance documentation when requested
  • No explanation of utilities or hidden conditions
  • A low headline price with important work left undefined

Lowest price versus best value

The lowest quote may be appropriate when the scopes are truly identical. Problems arise when one price includes root flare, access planning and cleanup while another covers only a quick pass across the central stump. Compare line by line before deciding.

Frequently asked questions

Should a stump grinding quote be written?

Yes. The written scope should identify the included stump and roots, grinding depth, access assumptions, chip handling, cleanup, utilities and payment terms.

Is the lowest stump grinding price always the best value?

No. Compare insurance, included depth, surface roots, access, chip handling, cleanup and change-order terms rather than the headline price alone.

Should I ask for proof of insurance?

Yes. Ask for current proof of liability coverage that applies to the scheduled work rather than relying only on a website badge.

Do I need an arborist before root grinding?

Not for every isolated stump from a removed tree, but significant roots connected to a living tree may warrant arborist review because cutting them can affect health or stability.
