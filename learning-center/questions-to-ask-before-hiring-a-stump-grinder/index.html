Hiring & Project Planning

Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Stump Grinder

The lowest number is not automatically the best quote. Compare access, depth, roots, chips, utilities, cleanup, insurance and the exact written scope.

Updated August 4, 2026 · First Coast homeowner guide

Quick answer: Before hiring, verify insurance, confirm the machine can reach the stump, require a written grinding depth, identify which roots are included, determine what happens to the chips, disclose private lines and understand payment and damage-responsibility terms.

1. Are you currently insured?

Ask for current proof of liability insurance and confirm the business name matches the company performing the work. A verbal statement is not the same as documentation. Insurance does not replace careful work, but it is an important protection when heavy equipment will operate near fences, pools, vehicles, buildings or landscaping.

2. What exactly is included in the written price?

The quote should identify every stump and state whether the root flare or outward surface roots are included. It should also explain grinding depth, access assumptions, chip handling, cleanup, soil or grading and any conditions that could change the price.

3. How deep will the stump be ground?

“Below grade” is too vague. The appropriate scope depends on whether the area will receive grass, mulch, a fence, a patio, pool work or construction. Ask for the intended depth or project outcome to be written into the quote.

4. Does the price include surface roots?

A wide root flare can create a much larger work area than the flat cut surface. Confirm whether visible roots are included and how far from the stump the operator plans to grind. Roots connected to a living tree require greater caution because cutting them may affect health or stability.

5. Can your machine reach the stump?

Measure the narrowest clear gate opening and show the entire route. Hinges, posts, tight turns, slopes, steps, air-conditioning units, soft ground and overhead obstacles can matter as much as the gate itself. Ask whether the selected machine is suitable for both the access and the stump size.

6. What happens to the wood chips?

Grinding creates a mixture of chips, sawdust and soil that can occupy more volume than the original stump. Confirm whether the material will be left, spread, piled or removed. Topsoil, final grading, seed and sod should not be assumed unless listed.

7. How are utilities and private lines handled?

Public utility markings may not identify private irrigation, pool plumbing, landscape lighting, septic components, invisible fencing or customer-installed electrical lines. Disclose known private lines and ask what responsibilities belong to the homeowner and contractor.

8. Can you work near a fence, pool or hardscape?

Ask how debris will be controlled and how the machine will be positioned. Limited clearance may prevent every portion of a stump from being reached without removing a fence panel or changing the project scope.

9. Is the estimate firm or preliminary?

Photo estimates can be efficient, but hidden metal, rocks, buried roots, soft ground or access that differs from the pictures may change the work. The company should explain when pricing becomes final and how scope changes are approved.

10. What are the payment terms?

Confirm deposit requirements, final payment timing, accepted payment methods and the process for added work. Avoid unclear agreements that require full payment before the scope is established.

11. What should happen after grinding?

Ask whether the goal is simply to reduce the stump or prepare the area for lawn restoration. Grass usually performs better after excess chips are removed, suitable topsoil is added and the area is graded. Some settlement can occur later as remaining roots decay.

12. Do recent reviews describe projects like mine?

Look for detailed reviews mentioning access, communication, cleanup, nearby structures and the number or size of stumps. A project with a narrow gate or pool enclosure is more relevant to your decision than a generic review saying only “great job.”

Frequently asked questions

Should I choose the cheapest stump grinding quote?

Not automatically. First compare grinding depth, root coverage, access, chip handling, cleanup, insurance and exclusions. Two prices may describe very different scopes.

Should the grinding depth be written down?

Yes. Written expectations reduce disputes and connect the work to the planned use of the area.

Is an arborist always needed?

Not for every isolated stump. Arborist input becomes more valuable when significant roots from a living tree may be cut or when tree stability is a concern.
