Hiring & Trust

Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Stump Grinder

A clear written scope matters more than a vague promise to “grind it below ground.”

Updated August 4, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Quick answer: Confirm insurance, included stumps and roots, depth, equipment access, chip handling, utilities, cleanup, payment and what happens if hidden conditions are found.

Is the company currently insured?

Ask for proof of liability coverage that is current for the scheduled work. Do not rely only on a website badge.

Which stump and roots are included?

The scope should identify each stump and say whether root flare or outward surface roots are included. “Stump grinding” does not automatically mean every visible root.

What depth is included?

Connect the depth to the future use: grass, mulch, fencing, hardscape or construction. A specific written scope is better than vague below-grade language.

What machine access is required?

Ask about minimum gate width, turns, slopes, steps and ground protection. The machine may fit through the gate but not around the next corner.

What happens to the chips?

Confirm whether chips are left, spread, piled or removed. Ask whether imported soil, grading, seed or sod are separate.

Who handles utilities and private lines?

Discuss public markings and private irrigation, pool plumbing, lighting, septic components and invisible fencing.

What are the payment and change-order terms?

The quote should explain payment timing and how unexpected rocks, metal, roots or access changes will be handled.

Hiring checklist

Should the quote be written?

Yes. Written depth, cleanup and access terms reduce misunderstandings.

Is the lowest price always the best value?

No. Compare the included scope, insurance, depth and cleanup rather than headline price alone.

Do I need an arborist?

Not for every isolated removed stump, but significant roots connected to a living tree may warrant arborist review.
