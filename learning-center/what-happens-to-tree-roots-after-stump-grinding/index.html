Roots and Grinding Depth

What Happens to Tree Roots After Stump Grinding?

Grinding removes the central obstruction, but it does not excavate the complete underground root system.

Updated August 5, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Written for lawn restoration, root planning and future construction decisions across Northeast Florida.

Quick answer: Most buried roots remain after stump grinding. They gradually lose strength and break down in the soil. Accessible surface roots can be included separately, but complete root removal requires excavation. Settlement may occur later as buried wood decomposes.

What the grinder reaches

The cutter works through the central stump, root flare and selected reachable roots. It does not follow every lateral root across the yard or remove the entire root ball.

Root areaTypical result after grinding
Central stumpReduced below the agreed grade.
Root flareOften included when visible and safely reachable.
Surface rootsMay be included separately when photographed and marked.
Deep rootsRemain underground unless excavated.
Distant lateral rootsUsually remain and decompose over time.

What happens underground over time

Once the tree is gone, the remaining roots no longer support the original canopy. Buried wood gradually decomposes. The rate varies with root size, moisture, soil, species and condition.

Why settlement can occur

Large roots occupied physical space in the soil. As they decay, voids can develop and the surface may settle. Lawn areas may need future top-dressing and regrading, especially where the root flare or major roots were large.

When remaining roots are usually acceptable

Future useRoot concern
Mulch bedRemaining roots are often acceptable beneath a landscaped area.
Grass or sodRoots can remain, but settlement and soil restoration should be expected.
Fence postsRoots may conflict with exact post locations.
Patio or drivewayBuried roots may affect excavation or base preparation.
Pool, foundation or trenchRoots inside the footprint may need targeted excavation.

Can roots keep growing?

Most roots from a dead or fully removed tree stop active growth. Some trees may produce sprouts from surviving root tissue, so regrowth concerns should be discussed separately rather than assuming the central stump alone controls every root response.

Surface roots connected to living trees

Roots from a living tree are different from leftover roots after tree removal. Major structural roots help anchor the tree and support water and nutrient movement. Cutting them can affect health or stability and may require arborist review.

When excavation is the better scope

  • Foundation or pool footprint
  • Utility trench or drainage installation
  • Fence-post location blocked by a major root
  • Structural hardscape requiring root-free excavation
  • Large root crown that must be physically removed

Frequently asked questions

Does stump grinding remove the root ball?

No. Grinding reduces the stump and reachable root flare in place while most of the underground root system remains.

Can the ground settle after stump grinding?

Yes. Soil can settle as buried roots and remaining wood break down, especially where large roots occupied significant volume.

Can roots keep growing after the tree is removed?

Most roots from a dead or fully removed tree stop active growth, though some species may produce sprouts from surviving tissue.

When are remaining roots a problem?

They may be a problem inside a foundation, pool, utility trench, fence-post location or other excavation footprint.
