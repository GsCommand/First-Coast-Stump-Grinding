Roots & Aftercare

What Happens to Tree Roots After Stump Grinding?

Grinding removes the stump and reachable flare—not the entire underground root network.

Updated August 4, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Quick answer: Most buried roots remain after grinding and gradually break down in the soil. Accessible surface roots may be included separately, but full root removal requires excavation.

What the grinder reaches

The cutter works in the stump area and can often reduce the central stump, root flare and selected visible roots. It does not follow every lateral root across the property.

What happens underground

Remaining roots lose their connection to the removed tree and slowly decompose. As this happens, the soil may settle and require future top-dressing, especially where large roots occupied significant volume.

Can roots keep growing?

Most roots from a dead or fully removed tree stop active growth. Some species can produce sprouts from surviving roots, so species and stump condition should be considered when regrowth is a concern.

When are remaining roots a problem?

They are often acceptable under lawns or mulch. They may be a problem inside a foundation, pool, utility trench, fence-post location or other excavation footprint.

Surface roots from living trees

Do not treat structural roots from a living tree like leftover stump roots. Cutting major roots can affect health or stability and may require arborist review.

Frequently asked questions

Does grinding remove the root ball?

No. It reduces the stump in place; the main underground root mass remains unless excavated.

Can the ground settle later?

Yes. Soil can settle as buried wood breaks down.
