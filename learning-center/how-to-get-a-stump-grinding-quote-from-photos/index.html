How to Get a Stump Grinding Quote From Photos

The best photo set shows the stump, its widest measurement, the complete equipment route and anything that could change the work.

Updated August 4, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Quick answer: Send a full stump photo, tape measure across the widest root flare, surface-root photos, gate width, route from the street, nearby obstacles, number of stumps, ZIP code and intended use afterward.

1. Photograph the entire stump

Take one wide photo showing the stump in relation to the yard. Then take close photos of the cut surface, root flare and exposed roots. Avoid sending only a tight close-up because it hides access and scale.

2. Measure the widest root flare

Lay a tape measure across the widest point at ground level. The root flare may extend beyond the flat cut surface, so measuring only the trunk can understate the work area.

3. Show every part of the access route

Photograph the driveway or street entry, side yard, gate, tight turns, slopes, steps and final approach to the stump. Measure the narrowest clear opening between posts, hinges and fixed obstacles.

4. Show nearby obstacles

Include fences, pools, pavers, patios, sheds, walls, air-conditioning units, irrigation equipment, landscape lighting and visible utility markers.

5. Explain what happens after grinding

Tell the estimator whether the area will receive sod, mulch, fencing, a patio, pool work or construction. The next use affects the appropriate depth and cleanup scope.

6. Number multiple stumps

Label each stump in the photos and provide a separate measurement for each. Include the total number and whether they share the same access route.

Why a photo estimate may change onsite

Buried metal, rocks, hidden roots, private lines, soft ground or access that differs from the photos can change the work. A photo estimate is only as reliable as the information provided.

Frequently asked questions

Can one photo be enough?

Usually not. A useful estimate needs scale, root flare and equipment access.

Should I use a shoe or can for scale?

A tape measure is better because it provides an actual dimension.

Do I need to photograph the gate?

Yes. Gate width and turns can determine which machine can reach the stump.
