Pricing and Estimates

How to Get a Stump Grinding Quote From Photos

A good photo estimate shows more than the stump. It shows size, root flare, access, obstacles and what you want to do with the area afterward.

Updated August 5, 2026 · First Coast field guide

Written for homeowners and property managers in Jacksonville, St. Johns County, Clay County and Nassau County.

Quick answer: Send three core views: the full stump, a tape measure across the widest root flare, and the complete equipment access route. Add close photos of surface roots, the gate opening, nearby fences or concrete, visible utilities and anything else that could change the work.

A useful stump grinding estimate depends on the same details a contractor would inspect onsite. The goal is not to make the stump look large or small. The goal is to show the true work area and whether a machine can reach it safely without surprises.

The complete photo-quote checklist

  • Full stump and surrounding yard
  • Tape measure across the widest visible root flare
  • Close photos of exposed surface roots
  • Gate opening measured at the narrowest clear point
  • Route from the street or driveway to the stump
  • Tight turns, slopes, steps and soft ground
  • Fences, pools, pavers, sheds and air-conditioning equipment
  • Visible utility markings, irrigation equipment and landscape lighting
  • Total number of stumps
  • ZIP code and intended use after grinding

How each photo affects the estimate

Photo or detailWhat it helps determine
Full stump viewOverall scale, surrounding clearance and whether roots extend beyond the trunk.
Widest measurementThe actual grinding area at ground level, including root flare.
Gate and access routeWhether narrow-access equipment can reach the work and how much setup is required.
Nearby obstaclesDebris-control needs and working clearance around fences, pools, walls and hardscape.
Future useWhether the scope should be planned for grass, mulch, fencing, hardscape or construction.

Measure the root flare, not only the trunk

The flat cut surface is often narrower than the visible root flare at ground level. Place the tape measure across the widest part of the stump and flare. A second measurement from a different angle can help when the stump is irregular.

A shoe, bucket or soda can gives only a rough sense of scale. A visible tape measure provides an actual dimension that can be compared across photos.

Photograph the complete access route

Start where equipment would leave the street or driveway and photograph every part of the route to the stump. Include the side yard, gate, narrow passages, turns, slopes, steps and the final approach.

In Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities, backyard access can be shaped by vinyl fences, pool enclosures, paver walkways, irrigation equipment and finished landscaping. The machine may fit through the gate but still be blocked by a tight turn or fixed obstacle farther inside the property.

Show obstacles before they become change orders

Include close photos of nearby concrete, fences, pool decks, walls, sheds, air-conditioning units, lighting, irrigation heads and visible utility markings. Private lines such as irrigation, pool plumbing, low-voltage lighting and invisible fencing may not appear on public utility markings, so they should be disclosed when known.

Tell the estimator what happens next

Planned useWhy it matters
Mulch bedA basic below-grade scope may be sufficient when the area will remain landscaped.
Grass or sodChip removal, suitable topsoil, grading and future settlement may need to be considered separately.
Fence installationThe exact post location and nearby roots may affect the requested scope.
Patio, pool or constructionStandard stump grinding is not the same as full excavation of the underground root system.

How to photograph multiple stumps

Number each stump in the photos and provide a separate measurement for each. Include one property overview showing where the stumps sit in relation to one another and whether they share the same access route. This supports route-efficient pricing and reduces the chance that a stump is omitted from the written scope.

Why a photo estimate can still change onsite

Photos cannot always reveal buried metal, rocks, deep root structure, private utilities, unstable soil or access conditions hidden behind the camera. A detailed photo set makes the estimate more reliable, but the final work still depends on actual site conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Can one photo be enough for a stump grinding quote?

Usually not. A useful estimate needs the stump, its true measurement, the access route and nearby obstacles.

Where should I place the tape measure?

Across the widest visible part of the stump and root flare at ground level, not only across the flat cut surface.

Should I photograph the gate and side yard?

Yes. The narrowest opening, turns, slopes and fixed obstacles can determine whether equipment can reach the stump.

Can a photo estimate change onsite?

Yes. Hidden metal, rocks, private lines, buried roots, soft ground or access conditions that differ from the photos can change the final scope.
