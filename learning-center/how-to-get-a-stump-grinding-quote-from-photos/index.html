HOW TO GET A STUMP GRINDING QUOTE FROM PHOTOS

The best photo set shows the stump, its widest measurement, the complete equipment route and anything that could change the work.

Home›Learning Center›Photo Quote Guide

Updated August 4, 2026 · First Coast Stump Grinding field guide

Quick answer: Send a full stump photo, a tape measure across the widest root flare, photos of surface roots, the gate width, the complete route from the street, nearby obstacles, the number of stumps, the ZIP code and what you plan to do with the area afterward.

The complete photo-quote checklist

PHOTO 01

The entire stump

Stand far enough away to show the whole stump and how it sits within the yard.

PHOTO 02

The widest measurement

Place a tape measure across the widest root flare at ground level—not only the flat cut surface.

PHOTO 03

Surface roots

Show every visible root extending beyond the stump, especially roots near concrete, fences or planting areas.

PHOTO 04

Gate and narrowest opening

Measure the clear opening between posts or hinges and show any tight turn immediately beyond it.

PHOTO 05

The entire access route

Photograph the path from the driveway or street through the side yard to the stump.

PHOTO 06

Nearby obstacles

Include pools, patios, pavers, fences, sheds, walls, air conditioners, irrigation and landscape lighting.

Why the root flare matters

The flat cut surface is not always the full grinding area. Mature stumps may widen considerably at ground level, and surface roots may extend beyond the trunk. Measuring only the top can create an estimate that understates the work.

How to measure the gate correctly

Measure the narrowest clear opening—not the advertised gate size. Account for hinges, latches, posts, air-conditioning units, steps, slopes, tight turns and overhead obstructions. A machine may pass through the gate but still be unable to make the turn beyond it.

Tell us what comes next

The intended use affects the project scope. Preparing for grass or mulch may differ from clearing a fence line, patio area, pool project or future construction zone. State the goal before the estimate is treated as final.

Future useInformation to include
Grass or sodDesired finish, chip removal expectations and whether topsoil or grading is needed.
Mulch or planting bedPlanting plans and whether the stump area needs to be lowered or leveled.
Fence installationProposed fence line, post locations and roots extending along the route.
Patio, pool or constructionContractor requirements and whether deeper excavation—not routine grinding—may be necessary.

How to photograph multiple stumps

Number each stump in the photos and provide a separate measurement for each. Include a wide property photo showing whether all stumps share the same equipment route. Shared access and setup may affect project efficiency.

Why a photo estimate may change onsite

A photo estimate is only as reliable as the information supplied. Buried metal, rocks, hidden roots, private irrigation or electrical lines, soft soil, steep slopes or access conditions that differ from the photos can change the work.

Frequently asked questions

Can one photo be enough?

Usually not. One close-up rarely shows scale, root flare, access and nearby obstacles.

Should I use a shoe or can for scale?

A tape measure is better because it provides an actual dimension that can be checked.

Do I need to photograph the gate?

Yes. Gate width, hinges and turns can determine whether the equipment can reach the stump.

Can photos provide a guaranteed final price?

They can often support a useful estimate, but final pricing may change when hidden conditions or access differ onsite.
