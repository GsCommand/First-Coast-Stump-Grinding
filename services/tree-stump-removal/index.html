KNOW THE DIFFERENCE

STUMP REMOVAL DOES NOT ALWAYS MEAN DIGGING OUT EVERY ROOT.

In residential work, “stump removal” often means grinding the stump and accessible root flare below grade so the area can be restored, landscaped or prepared for a new project.

Full excavation can require larger machinery, more soil disturbance and additional restoration. The right solution depends on whether the area will receive sod, a fence, a pool, a patio, structural work or simple landscaping.

  • Below-grade grinding for lawn restoration
  • Deeper project-specific grinding where practical
  • Access and obstacle review before scheduling
  • Clear explanation of what grinding will and will not remove
WHEN REMOVAL MATTERS

FAQ

TREE STUMP REMOVAL QUESTIONS

Is stump grinding the same as full stump excavation?

No. Grinding reduces the stump and accessible root flare below grade. Full excavation removes more of the root ball but causes greater soil disturbance and usually requires larger equipment.

Can the area be replanted afterward?

Often yes, but soil preparation may be needed. Replanting a large tree in the exact location can require additional root and soil work.

Will all underground roots disappear?

No. Grinding does not remove every underground root. Remaining roots generally decay over time.

Can you remove a stump before fence installation?

Yes. Share the proposed fence line and post locations so the grinding scope can be planned around the installation.
