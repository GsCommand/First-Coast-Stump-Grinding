ROOTS FROM A REMOVED TREE

WHAT ROOT GRINDING CAN ADDRESS.

After a tree is removed, its root flare and surface roots may remain above grade. These roots can interfere with mowing, sod, mulch beds, fence installation, walkways and landscape grading.

  • Root flare surrounding a removed stump
  • Exposed roots creating mower or trip hazards
  • Roots blocking sod or planting-bed preparation
  • Accessible roots along proposed fence lines
  • Surface roots in future landscape or path areas

Grinding targets visible and reachable material. It does not remove the entire underground root system.

ROOTS FROM A LIVING TREE

NOT EVERY EXPOSED ROOT SHOULD BE CUT.

Large roots connected to a living tree can contribute to water and nutrient movement, anchoring and structural stability. Grinding or severing a significant root may weaken the tree or increase failure risk.

Roots close to the trunk, roots on the side of a lean, or large structural roots may warrant review by an ISA Certified Arborist before work proceeds. First Coast does not make a blanket promise that every living-tree root can safely be removed.

FAQ

ROOT GRINDING QUESTIONS

Can exposed surface roots be ground?

Roots from a removed tree can often be included. Roots attached to a living tree require evaluation because removing them may affect health or stability.

Does root grinding remove the whole root system?

No. It targets accessible roots within the work area and does not excavate every buried root.

Can roots beneath concrete be ground?

Not through intact hardscape. Concrete, pavers or other surfaces normally must be removed before covered roots can be reached.

Can root grinding stop future settling?

Not completely. Buried roots may continue decomposing, and soil can settle later. Construction areas may require excavation or engineering beyond routine grinding.
